# Two ideas from the engineer, 2026-09-11

Written down so they are not lost; neither enters the app before the hackathon entry is
submitted (rule from `README.md`). Both belong in the "what comes next" part of the entry.

## 1. Closeout files things, the engineer stays in charge of the folder

Closeout may put a document where it thinks it belongs (a received certificate into the
right discipline's closeout folder, a contractor's photo against the right item). But the
engineer can always override it: drag it to another folder, rename it (by hand or by saying
"call that the sprinkler test certificate"), or say "move that to Mechanical" in the spoken
conversation and have the app carry it out.

- Every automatic placement is shown as "filed by Closeout" until the engineer touches it.
- Moving is one action, undoable, and shows up in the project's history.
- Spoken moves go through the same confirm step as every other change (yes / no).

Why: the office already has a folder per project and a way of working. The app should save
the walking, not take the folder away.

## 2. The "who else needs to know" check on drawings

The story behind it: the plumbing designer moved a fixture, the mechanical designer
changed their layout to suit, but the electrical designer was never told, so the outlet
stayed where it was. The clash was found on site and cost the client a large change order.

What Closeout could do when a revised sheet arrives for one discipline:

1. Find the other disciplines' sheets that cover the same area (same floor, same grid).
2. Say which ones have not been revised since, and who would normally need to know.
3. Put a note on the project: "Plumbing P-201 rev 3 received; Electrical E-201 unchanged
   since rev 1, covers the same units." The engineer decides whether to send it on.

4. When a sheet arrives as a new revision of one the app already holds, compare the two and
   list what changed (moved, added, removed, renumbered), so the engineer reviews the
   difference and not the whole sheet again. Each revision is kept; nothing is overwritten.

It does not try to prove a clash from the drawings. It points at the sheets that were not
looked at again, which is the thing that was actually missed. That is doable with the
sheet index the app already builds; a real geometry clash check is a much bigger job and
is not promised.

## 3. Where the photo was taken, and the walk itself

The wish: know which floor and where the engineer was for each photo, and be able to see
the walk afterwards.

- Outdoors, the phone's GPS is good enough to tell which building on a site the engineer
  is at, so the app can pre-select it. Indoors, GPS does not work and does not know floors.
- The honest indoor version is what the app already half does: the engineer picks the
  floor and pins the photo on the plan. From those pins, in time order, the app can draw
  the walk on the plan (a trail from pin to pin with times), and show which rooms or units
  were never visited. That needs no new sensors and is doable soon.
- A "start point" mode (tap where you are, then let the phone's step counter and compass
  guess your position) is possible but drifts within a few metres and would need testing on
  a real walk before it is trusted. Not promised.

## 4. One inbox per project, many doors in (added Fri evening)

The wish: every document ever received stays in the app, old issues included, and the
contractor can send an update in whatever way they already use: the link, an email with
attachments, a text message, WhatsApp. Photos and words arrive together ("fixed item 3, see
photo").

What is true today:

- Nothing is ever deleted. Every issue of every drawing set stays in the project folder; the
  Documents tab lists older issues under the current one, and the compare feature depends on
  them being kept.
- The contractor's link accepts any file type. Photos and PDFs are looked at; anything else
  is kept as sent. Byte-identical repeats are marked as duplicates.
- Gaps: the contractor's words are not kept, only their files; and a document sent through
  the link (say a certificate) lands as review evidence, not in the project's document tree.

The shape to build after the entry is submitted:

- One "Inbox" per project. Every arrival is one record: who, when, which door it came in
  by, the words, the files. The engineer files it from there (the Move panel from idea 1),
  or Closeout suggests a place and marks it "filed by Closeout".
- Doors are thin adapters onto that one inbox, added one at a time:
  1. The link (built).
  2. Email: a project address that forwards into the app; attachments become files, the
     body becomes the words. Needs the office's domain and inbound mail on AWS.
  3. Text message with pictures: Twilio, days of work.
  4. WhatsApp: Twilio's WhatsApp channel needs Meta business approval first, which takes
     weeks; do this last.
- Whatever the door, the contractor never needs an account, and nothing leaves the office
  without the engineer.

Until the doors exist, an email or WhatsApp attachment is saved by the engineer and dropped
into the project by hand; that already works.
